### Answer

In 1789, Paris was at the center of what became known as **the First Wave of the French Revolution**. The events that took place there marked a radical shift in European history and society from absolutist monarchy to popular sovereignty. Here are some of the key developments:

*   **Storming of the Bastille:** On May 5th (and sometimes cited early July/August depending on historical versions), the Parisian people stormed the fortress, demanding freedom for their slaves and an end to arbitrary rule by the King's forces.
*   **Formation of a Government in Versailles:** Following this event, representatives met at a palace near Paris (**Versailles**) which they later moved into as the National Assembly (June 1789) formed itself from these gatherings. This body sought to replace royal authority with one based on law and representation for all citizens.
*   **Flight of King Louis XVI:** In March/April 1789, members of the French nobility arrested the King in Paris. Although he escaped arrest later that month (**Flight**), his imprisonment became a central symbol of monarchy's corruption to be overthrown. This incident led to political unrest across France and culminated in **September** when Louis XVI was forced by popular pressure into exile or execution depending on historical interpretation, but specifically for this year the events were driven by mass protests against him.
*   **Declaration of Rights:** In August/July 1789, revolutionary groups began to draft a constitution declaring fundamental rights (freedom, religion, property) and establishing that these would be protected by law rather than arbitrary royal decree.

The combination of the formation of the National Assembly at Versailles (often linked with Parisian gatherings), the storming of key institutions like the Bastille, and the Declaration itself created an atmosphere where political change became inevitable throughout France. This event set in motion a movement that would eventually lead to changes across Europe during this decade known as **The French Revolution**.
